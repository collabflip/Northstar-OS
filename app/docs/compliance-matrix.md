# Northstar SellerOS — Canadian Compliance Research Brief (Ontario-first)
**Source:** Wave A compliance research agent, 2026-08-02. Primary/official sources: laws-lois.justice.gc.ca, crtc.gc.ca, fintrac-canafe.canada.ca, ontario.ca e-Laws, reco.on.ca, priv.gc.ca, ohrc.on.ca. Items marked VERIFY need counsel confirmation. Default review cadence: semi-annual + event-driven on amendments. **43 rules.** This brief states the **required** control per rule; the **implemented** status of each control (enforced / partial / declared) lives in `docs/compliance-control-matrix.md`.

## AREA 1 — CASL (S.C. 2010, c. 23)

**CASL-01 — CEM definition & prohibition.** Source: CASL ss.1,6 (laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/). Federal, in force 2014-07-01. Requirement: prohibited to send a CEM without consent + form/content compliance; CEM = electronic message with any purpose of encouraging participation in commercial activity (incl. offers to buy/sell/lease real property, promoting a person who does). Control: pre-send classifier gate; any outbound email/SMS/DM promoting listings/services/agent is treated as CEM and must pass consent+form checklist; transactional replies logged with justification. Tests: (1) listing alert to opted-in lead passes; (2) "checking in" promo to scraped address blocked; (3) SMS promo with expired implied consent blocked. Escalation: compliance officer. Confidence: High.

**CASL-02 — Express consent.** Source: CASL s.10(1)–(8). Request must set out purposes + prescribed identification (name, on-behalf-of, mailing address + phone/email/web). Express consent doesn't expire until withdrawn. Control: consent-capture stores consent text, purpose, ID block, timestamp, channel, source; unchecked-by-default opt-in only (pre-checked = invalid per CRTC). Tests: opt-in with full ID block valid; pre-checked box flagged; verbal consent queues written verification. Escalation: compliance officer + counsel. High.

**CASL-03 — Implied consent: EBR.** Source: CASL s.10(9),(10),(13). Implied via purchase/lease/contract within **2 years**, or inquiry/application within **6 months**; lapses at window close; recipient can end anytime. Control: `implied_consent_basis` + `window_expiry` per contact; scheduler blocks CEMs at/after expiry absent express consent; expiry triggers re-confirmation campaign (itself needs basis). Tests: closed 14mo ago OK; inquiry 7mo ago blocked; purchase 25mo ago blocked. Escalation: compliance officer. High.

**CASL-04 — Implied consent: published address / referral.** Source: CASL s.10(9)(b)–(d),(11)–(12). Conspicuously published address w/o no-CEM statement + relevance to role; referral basis allows **one** message only and must name the referrer. Control: referral template requires referrer name rendered; hard-cap 1 send per address on referral basis; published-address claims need stored URL/screenshot + relevance note. Tests: one referral email OK; second blocked; scraped-from-"no solicitations" blocked. Escalation: compliance officer. High.

**CASL-05 — Sender identification.** Source: CASL s.10(1) + ECP Regulations SOR/2013-221 s.2. Every CEM identifies sender (+ on-behalf-of), mailing address + phone-with-voicemail/email/web; contact info valid ≥**60 days** post-send; SMS may link to a page. Control: template footer auto-injects sender + brokerage legal name, address, monitored phone/email; send fails if footer vars empty or liveness check fails. Tests: complete footer sends; unresolved placeholder blocked; decommissioned mailbox flagged. Escalation: compliance officer. High.

**CASL-06 — Unsubscribe + 10 business days.** Source: CASL s.11. Clear/prominent unsubscribe, readily performed, no cost, same electronic means (or link valid ≥60 days); honour without delay, max **10 business days**. Control: one-click unsubscribe + list-unsubscribe header; real-time central suppression list (global, permanent until re-consent); hard-block sends to suppressed; latency report (<24h target). Tests: unsubscribed excluded next day; manual re-add rejected; broken unsubscribe link fails pre-send. Escalation: auto-alert at 5 business days. High.

**CASL-07 — Burden of proof / record-keeping.** Source: CASL s.13; CRTC 2012-548/2012-549 guidance. Sender bears onus of proving consent; expect documented policies + records of how/when consent obtained. Control: immutable consent ledger (who, exact text/version, when UTC, where form/page/version, how); retained indefinitely; per-contact evidence bundle exportable. Tests: bundle in <5 min; "consent: yes" w/o source = no consent; implied links underlying transaction. Escalation: CRTC complaint → counsel + broker of record. High.

**CASL-08 — Penalties.** Source: CASL ss.20–22. AMPs up to **$1M/violation individuals, $10M/violation organizations**; vicarious + officer/director liability; private right of action never in force. Control: tenant-wide send limits, mandatory pre-checks, quarterly consent-hygiene report to broker of record, named CASL owner. Tests: side-tool sends detected/quarantined; list export to personal email → DLP alert; suppression coverage 100%. Escalation: broker of record + counsel. High.

## AREA 2 — National DNCL & CRTC UTR

**DNCL-01 — Registration/subscription.** Source: CRTC UTR Part I; lnnte-dncl.gc.ca. Telemarketers (and clients) must register before telemarketing and subscribe to DNCL for called area codes; registration free since 2022-04-01 but mandatory. Control: outbound calling disabled until registration account ID + subscription configured; renewal alerts at 30 days. Tests: unregistered → calls blocked; expired subscription locks dialer. Escalation: compliance officer. High.

**DNCL-02 — Scrub + 31-day grace.** Source: UTR Part I. No telemarketing calls to DNCL-registered numbers; scrub list must be ≤**31 days** old; DNCL registrations never expire. Control: nightly scrub; dialer refuses if list age >31d; matches suppressed permanently + logged. Tests: registered 40d ago suppressed; stale 45d list blocks all dialing; registered yesterday callable (grace, flagged). Escalation: compliance officer. High.

**DNCL-03 — Internal DNC: 14 days, retain 3y+14d.** Source: UTR Part II. Internal do-not-call list mandatory; honour requests within **14 days** (recommend immediate); retain number **3 years and 14 days**. Control: one-click DNC flag propagating everywhere; retention timer auto-calculated; permanent option encouraged. Tests: mid-call "stop calling" excluded next day; flagged number in later campaign blocked + incident; never auto-purged early. Escalation: compliance officer → broker of record on repeats. High.

**DNCL-04 — Calling hours.** Source: UTR Part II. Telemarketing only **9:00–21:30 weekdays, 10:00–18:00 Sat/Sun, in the called consumer's local timezone** (same for unsolicited faxes). Control: per-timezone window enforcement resolved from number/address; hard block outside; ambiguous timezone → most restrictive or manual review. Tests: 9:45am Toronto→BC blocked; 5:30pm Sunday ON allowed; 9:45pm weekday blocked. Escalation: deterministic; anomalies → compliance officer. High.

**DNCL-05 — Call-start identification.** Source: UTR Part II. At call start identify on-whose-behalf, telemarketer if different, purpose; on request provide reachable number (+address/fax for telemarketers); caller ID must display a reachable number. Control: mandatory call-start script (enforced in any auto-dial/AI flow); caller ID = monitored number; script-adherence spot checks. Tests: ID in first 15s pass; AI voice skipping purpose fails certification; unreachable caller ID blocks campaign. Escalation: QA → compliance officer. High.

**DNCL-06 — Exemptions incl. EBR durations.** Source: UTR Part I. DNCL doesn't apply to EBR: purchase/lease within **18 months** or inquiry/application within **6 months**; other exemptions (charities, political, newspapers, surveys, B2B, express referral); exempt calls still follow Telemarketing Rules (hours/ID/internal DNC). Control: per-contact exemption registry (basis+expiry); dialer checks DNCL + exemption validity; expiry reverts to DNCL. Tests: closed 12mo ago on DNCL callable; inquiry 8mo on DNCL blocked; EBR at 9:45pm blocked by hours. Escalation: compliance officer. High.

**DNCL-07 — Abandoned calls / ADAD.** Source: UTR Part II. Abandonment ≤**5%/month/campaign**; abandoned call connects within 2s to recorded ID message; ADAD prerecorded solicitation to consumers generally prohibited absent express consent; sequential dialing to emergency lines prohibited. Control: abandon-rate telemetry + auto-pause at 5%; AI/prerecorded voice solicitation disabled by default; consent-based robocall requires express-consent evidence. Tests: 5.1% auto-pause; AI cold-call pitch blocked; compliant 2s message. Escalation: compliance officer + counsel before any voice automation. High. **Note: treat AI-voice outreach as presumptively prohibited.**

## AREA 3 — PIPEDA

**PIPEDA-01 — 10 principles.** Source: PIPEDA s.4 + Schedule 1 (laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/). Accountability, identifying purposes, consent, limiting collection, limiting use/disclosure/retention, accuracy, safeguards, openness, individual access, challenging compliance. Control: every data field mapped to declared purpose; named privacy officer; plain-language policy; access/challenge workflows with 30-day SLA. Tests: unmapped field blocks deploy; access export in 30 days; indefinite retention flagged. Escalation: privacy officer → OPC. High.

**PIPEDA-02 — Consent & purpose limitation.** Source: s.6.1, s.5(3), Sch.1 cl.4.2–4.3. Valid consent requires reasonable understanding; purposes identified at/before collection; only purposes a reasonable person would consider appropriate; new purposes need fresh consent. Control: purpose registry bound to consent records; using CRM data for AI training/enrichment = new-purpose gate; consent language versioning. Tests: consented transaction use OK; undisclosed third-party AI use blocked; granular marketing vs transaction consent. Escalation: privacy officer + counsel. High.

**PIPEDA-03 — Minimization, retention, safeguards.** Source: Sch.1 cl.4.4, 4.5, 4.7. Collect only what's necessary, by fair/lawful means; retain only as long as necessary then securely destroy/anonymize; safeguards proportional to sensitivity. Control: field-necessity review (no SIN/DOB unless FINTRAC/TRESA requires); RBAC, encryption at rest/in transit, audit logs; retention schedule + legal-hold override aligned to FINTRAC 5y/RECO. Tests: ID doc destroyed after 5y; unencrypted export blocked; inactive lead anonymized. Escalation: privacy officer. High.

**PIPEDA-04 — Breach RROSH reporting.** Source: ss.10.1–10.3 (in force 2018-11-01). Breach posing **real risk of significant harm**: report to OPC as soon as feasible, notify individuals, notify organizations that can reduce risk. Significant harm incl. humiliation, reputational damage, financial loss, identity theft; assess sensitivity × probability. Control: IR runbook with RROSH template, decision log either way, OPC + individual notification templates, 72h internal SLA, incident register. Tests: financial docs exfiltrated → report+notify; lost encrypted laptop → documented negative; no-assessment decision flagged. Escalation: privacy officer → counsel → OPC. High.

**PIPEDA-05 — Breach records 24 months; offence $100k.** Source: s.10.3 + SOR/2018-64 s.6; offence s.28. Keep records of EVERY breach (regardless of RROSH) ≥**24 months**, sufficient for OPC verification; knowing contravention = offence up to **$100,000**. Control: permanent incident register (date, nature, data, assessment, notifications, remediation); exportable on demand. Tests: minor breach logged; record deleted at 12mo = audit failure; OPC spot-check produced same day. Escalation: privacy officer. High.

**PIPEDA-06 — Cross-border accountability/transparency.** Source: Sch.1 cl.4.1.3 + OPC 2009 cross-border guidelines. Accountability follows data to foreign processors (contractual comparable protection); transparency: individuals informed data may be processed abroad and accessible to foreign courts/LEO. Control: vendor registry with processing jurisdiction; DPAs with comparable-protection clauses; policy discloses cross-border; risk assessment before US-subject processor (CLOUD Act). Tests: new AI vendor DPA + policy update; unregistered offshore API blocked; disclosures state US possibility. Escalation: privacy officer + counsel. High (guidance-based transparency — note as such).

**PIPEDA-07 — Substantially similar provincial laws.** Source: PIPEDA s.26(2)(b); QC CQLR P-39.1 (Law 25, phased 2022–2024); AB/BC PIPAs. Quebec: privacy officer, PIAs (incl. before communicating PI outside Quebec), manifest consent, incident reporting to CAI + individuals, 5y incident register, automated-decision transparency; AMPs greater of $10M/2% worldwide turnover, penal to $25M/4%. Alberta mandatory RROSH breach notification; BC guidance-based. Control: province-of-residence tagging; Quebec data triggers Law 25 profile; Ontario-first scope. Tests: QC→US LLM blocked pending PIA; AB breach → AB OIPC notice; QC-under-PIPEDA-only flagged. Escalation: privacy officer + QC counsel. High (verify details if expanding).

## AREA 4 — FINTRAC / PCMLTFA (real estate)

**FIN-01 — Reporting entity scope.** Source: PCMLTFA s.5(i),(i.1); PCMLTFR SOR/2002-184 s.1(2); fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/re-ed/real-eng. Real estate brokers/sales representatives and developers (5+ new units or 1+ commercial/multi-unit per year) are reporting entities. Control: platform assumes brokerage is reporting entity; FINTRAC artifacts first-class per deal. Tests: resale obligations active; developer 6 units active; software vendor itself not a reporting entity. Escalation: compliance officer. High.

**FIN-02 — Compliance program.** Source: PCMLTFA s.9.6; PCMLTFR s.156. Appoint compliance officer; written policies/procedures; documented risk assessment; ongoing training; effectiveness review ≥ every **2 years**. Control: artifact repository, training log, 2-year review scheduler (90/60/30 alerts), versioned policies. Tests: review alerts fire; untrained agent flagged; policy updated post-amendment. Escalation: compliance officer → broker of record. High.

**FIN-03 — Client identification/verification (incl. unrepresented from 2025-10-01).** Source: PCMLTFR ss.102–112; FINTRAC methods guidance. Verify identity of individuals / confirm existence of entities for receipt-of-funds or client-info records using prescribed methods (govt photo ID, credit-file, dual-process), authentic/valid/current; **from Oct 1, 2025 extends to unrepresented parties**; at time of triggering transaction. Control: IDV workflow at offer/deposit + any unrepresented-party interaction; method/document/date captured; milestones blocked until verified. Tests: deposit → same-day IDV; expired passport rejected; unrepresented buyer post-Oct-2025 → auto task. Escalation: compliance officer. High on substance; VERIFY in-force text.

**FIN-04 — Receipt of funds records.** Source: PCMLTFR s.1(2), s.104. Record per amount received: date; provider name/address/DOB/occupation (or entity details); amount (cash portion); method; currencies; exchange rates; account numbers; other parties; reference numbers; **purpose**. Control: deposit intake form enforces full field set; incomplete cannot finalize; immutable on deal file. Tests: full capture incl. occupation; missing purpose blocked; one record per receipt. Escalation: compliance officer. High.

**FIN-05 — Third-party determination.** Source: PCMLTFR ss.105, 109. Reasonable measures to determine if funds provider acts for a third party; if so record prescribed third-party info; measures include asking client, open-source checks. Control: mandatory "acting for third party? Y/N/unknown" step; "unknown" requires measures-taken log. Tests: gifting parent → completed; skipped checkbox blocks; unknown needs note. Escalation: compliance officer; suspicious patterns → STR evaluation. High.

**FIN-06 — PEP/HIO screening.** Source: PCMLTFA s.9.3; PCMLTFR ss.2(1),105–106. Reasonable measures to determine PEFP/PEDP/HIO + prescribed family; if PEFP (or risk-positive PEDP/HIO) in transactions ≥**$100,000**: senior management approval + reasonable measures on source of funds + enhanced monitoring. Control: screening at intake, determination record, SM-approval workflow ≥$100k with source-of-funds docs. Tests: ambassador buyer flagged; missing approval blocks deposit; negative determination recorded. Escalation: compliance officer + senior management. High on framework; VERIFY timing nuances.

**FIN-07 — STR + anti-tipping-off.** Source: PCMLTFA ss.7,8; SOR/2001-317. Reasonable grounds to suspect ML/TF (completed OR attempted) → STR to FINTRAC **as soon as practicable** (regulations have prescribed 30-day outer bound — VERIFY current wording); s.8 prohibits disclosing STR existence/contents in a way that could prejudice an investigation. Control: STR workflow visible ONLY to fintrac_officer; client-facing UI shows nothing; access audited; ML/TF indicator checklist in deal review. Tests: third-party cash deposit → flag → STR, agent UI unchanged; agent tells client → violation event; attempted transaction still evaluated. Escalation: compliance officer sole decision-maker. High.

**FIN-08 — 5-year retention; LCTR/LVCTR.** Source: PCMLTFA s.6; PCMLTFR. Prescribed records retained ≥**5 years**. LCTR for **$10,000+ cash** (24h aggregation), submit within **15 days** (VERIFY current window); LVCTR **$10,000+ virtual currency**, **5 working days**. Control: cash/VC triggers with 24h aggregation; auto-draft reports; retention clock + legal hold; destruction log. Tests: $12k cash → LCTR; two $6k/24h aggregated; purge at 4y blocked. Escalation: compliance officer. High on thresholds/retention.

## AREA 5 — Ontario TRESA 2002 & RECO (Phase 2 in force 2023-12-01; O. Reg. 567/05; Code of Ethics O. Reg. 365/22)

**TRESA-01 — Registration & terminology.** Source: TRESA ss.1,4; RECO. No trading unless registered; categories salesperson/broker employed by brokerage; "agent"/"REALTOR®" not categories; "sales representative"/"customer" retired; client vs **self-represented party (SRP)**. Control: directory stores RECO number/category/brokerage; outputs render registered name+category; periodic check vs RECO public register. Tests: correct signature renders; "licensed agent" in ad flagged; lapsed registration locks trade functions. Escalation: broker of record. High.

**TRESA-02 — Broker of record.** Source: O. Reg. 567/05 (VERIFY subsection). Brokerage must designate broker of record responsible for compliance, supervision, trust accounts; remuneration flows through brokerage only. Control: org chart names BOR; commission flows via brokerage accounts; BOR dashboards see AI comms + blocks. Tests: BOR sees blocked sends; direct-to-agent payment blocked; trust deposit to brokerage trust. Escalation: BOR is statutory point. High (verify subsection).

**TRESA-03 — RECO Information Guide before services.** Source: O. Reg. 567/05 s.13(3)–(4) (VERIFY). Before any services/assistance (clients or SRPs), ensure person receives RECO Information Guide and explain contents; residential+commercial, buy+sell. Control: first-substantive-contact trigger sends/links current Guide, records delivery + explanation before service workflows (showings, CMAs, drafting) unlock; version pinned. Tests: Guide acknowledged → showing booked; CMA before Guide blocked; lease clients gated too. Escalation: broker of record. High (verify numbering).

**TRESA-04 — SRP restrictions.** Source: TRESA + O. Reg. 567/05 + RECO SRP guidance. SRP = party not a client of any brokerage. Registrant may provide only assistance incidental to services to their own client; must NOT provide SRP services/opinions/advice; must clarify no representation + encourage independent representation + Guide. Control: SRP-flagged contacts: assistant shares only factual client-serving info (logistics, property facts); blocked from advice/opinions/pricing/negotiation/form help; SRP disclosure auto-inserted; acknowledgment captured. Tests: showing time OK; "should I offer $950k?" declined + logged; drafting offer for SRP blocked. Escalation: broker of record. High (verify regulation text).

**TRESA-05 — Designated vs multiple representation.** Source: TRESA DR provisions; RECO Bulletin 3.4. DR: different designated registrants represent different clients in same trade, duties flow to designated registrant; otherwise multiple representation requires written consent from EVERY client after disclosure; designated registrants must not share client confidences. Control: deal graph detects same-brokerage opposing clients; representation records store model + consents; information barriers between DR files; consent-deficient MR blocks engagement. Tests: DR info barrier holds; MR without consents blocked; revoked consent triggers conflict workflow. Escalation: broker of record. High (verify mechanics vs bulletin).

**TRESA-06 — Advertising identification/prominence.** Source: O. Reg. 567/05 s.36 area (VERIFY); RECO Bulletin 5.1. All advertising (incl. social, AI-generated) must identify registrant by registered name, category (salesperson/broker — "sales representative" not permitted), and brokerage name **prominently** (not less prominent than registrant's); team names must not obscure brokerage. Control: templates enforce ID block; generator refuses marketing copy lacking name+category+brokerage prominence; pre-publish linter all channels. Tests: compliant IG post passes; team-name-tiny-brokerage fails; nickname fails. Escalation: broker of record. High (verify subsection).

**TRESA-07 — No false/misleading advertising.** Source: TRESA s.32 area (VERIFY); O. Reg. 365/22. No false/misleading/deceptive statements in advertising/representations — inaccurate property claims, unsubstantiated superlatives, misleading price/sold claims, incl. AI listing remarks. Control: generator cross-checks claims vs structured listing data; superlatives/statistics need source field; status claims verified vs board data. Tests: invented ravine backing flagged; matching "3-bedroom" passes; "#1 agent" w/o metric blocked. Escalation: broker of record. High (verify section).

**TRESA-08 — Offer handling.** Source: O. Reg. 567/05 (VERIFY); RECO Bulletin 4.1. Convey ALL written offers promptly (no suppression); client instructions in writing; competing offers: disclose NUMBER as required/directed; CONTENT/terms not disclosed except where seller directs in writing (TRESA Phase-2 open-offer process); never leak confidential client info. Control: offer intake logs every written offer + auto-notifies client; AI can never reveal competing terms; count disclosure follows recorded instruction; content-sharing disabled absent signed written direction; confidential-strategy fields access-scoped. Tests: low offer still presented; cross-side AI query refused + logged; signed direction unlocks content-sharing for that trade only. Escalation: broker of record immediately. High (verify consent mechanics).

**TRESA-09 — Open-house/showing conduct.** Source: RECO guidance + O. Reg. 365/22 (composite, guidance-based). Visitors commonly SRPs: identify as registrant for the seller, Guide before substantive assistance, no advice to SRPs, protect seller property (attendance logs, supervision); sign-in sheets comply with privacy law (purpose disclosed, secured). Control: open-house module: QR sign-in + privacy notice + separate unchecked marketing opt-in (CASL), auto Guide delivery, attendee log, SRP-mode kiosk guardrails. Tests: sign-in w/o marketing box → no consent; kiosk refuses "lowest they'll take"; Guide logged per attendee. Escalation: broker of record. Moderate-high.

## AREA 6 — Human rights / fair housing

**HR-01 — CHRA grounds.** Source: CHRA ss.3,5,6,12 (laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/H-6/). Federal scope only. Grounds: race, national/ethnic origin, colour, religion, age, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity/expression, marital status, family status, genetic characteristics, disability, pardoned conviction. s.6 housing discrimination; s.12 discriminatory notices. Control: shared prohibited-grounds taxonomy as configuration feeding content linter. High.

**HR-02 — Ontario Code housing grounds.** Source: HRC ss.1,2(1),3 (ontario.ca/laws/statute/90h19). Equal treatment in accommodation w/o discrimination because of race, ancestry, place of origin, colour, ethnic origin, citizenship, creed, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression, age, marital status, family status, disability, **receipt of public assistance** (accommodation); s.1 services; s.3 contracts. Control: never filter/rank/steer on protected grounds or proxies; discriminatory client instructions refused + logged. Tests: "no tenants on OW" refused + education; ethnic-neighbourhood request refused; accessibility request → accommodation workflow. Escalation: broker of record + counsel. High.

**HR-03 — Discriminatory advertising.** Source: HRC s.13; CHRA s.12 analogue. No publishing/displaying/circulating notices/ads indicating discrimination or intent to infringe (incl. family-status proxies, "adults only", public-assistance screening language) — applies to listing remarks and AI-drafted copy. Control: NLG linter with grounds+proxy lexicon on all property descriptions/ads/buyer criteria; flagged text needs human rewrite; "ideal for singles/no children" auto-flagged. Tests: "not suitable for kids" blocked; "Christian home preferred" blocked; transit/2-bed/pet-friendly passes. Escalation: broker of record. High.

**HR-04 — Steering/differential treatment.** Source: HRC s.1, s.11; OHRC policy; CHRA s.5. No steering on protected grounds, no differential effort, no relaying discriminatory preferences; constructive discrimination prohibited absent bona fide justification + accommodation to undue hardship. Control: assistant refuses demographic-coded instructions; neighbourhood descriptions restricted to objective amenities (never demographics); service-quality telemetry for differential patterns. Tests: coded instruction refused + incident; ethnic-makeup summary blocked; amenity summary passes. Escalation: broker of record + counsel. High (steering guidance policy-based).

**HR-05 — Accommodation + reprisal.** Source: HRC ss.8,11(2),17; CHRA ss.14.1,15(2),59. No reprisal for asserting rights; duty to accommodate to undue hardship (accessible formats, mobility-aware showings, translation). Control: accommodation intake tracked to completion; anti-reprisal flag blocks complaint-linked service termination. Tests: accessible showing logged; auto-archive after HRTO mention blocked; large-print docs. Escalation: broker of record; disputes → counsel. High.

## Top-10 AI-assistant compliance risks
1. CASL violations via automated outreach (no consent/unsubscribe) — AMPs to $10M. 2. Implied-consent expiry mismanagement (2y/6mo windows). 3. DNCL violations incl. AI/robocalling (hours 9:00–21:30/10:00–18:00 local; ADAD effectively prohibited). 4. STR tipping-off — strict need-to-know. 5. Missed FINTRAC duties in fast deals (IDV, receipt-of-funds, third-party, PEP, LCTR; unrepresented parties from 2025-10-01). 6. SRP boundary breaches (AI advising unrepresented parties). 7. Offer-handling integrity (suppression/leak across sides incl. DR barriers). 8. Discriminatory outputs (steering, demographic descriptions, family-status/public-assistance proxies). 9. Privacy leakage (LLM/CRM vendors without s.6.1 disclosure, cross-border transparency, RROSH gaps, 24-month breach register). 10. Advertising identity/prominence defects + AI-invented property claims.
